What Rosa is

Rosa is a password helper you use by text message. You text Rosa at 1-864-777-8711, and Rosa replies with a link where you can save or view part of a password for a website.

Rosa is built for people who use the same password everywhere, or who don’t use a password manager at all. The goal is a tool easy enough to actually use that is a real improvement on reusing one password. Rosa isn’t trying to be the most secure password manager available, and it isn’t meant to replace one you already use well.

Rosa is offered in the United States. To use Rosa, you must be at least 18 years old and the owner or authorized user of the mobile number you text from.

Use Rosa the way it’s designed

Rosa works best, and is safest, when each password has two parts: Rosa’s part, a unique part for each website that you save with Rosa, and your private part, which you remember and add yourself. Never give your private part to Rosa.

  • Don’t save a complete password with Rosa, and never text a password or any part of one. Save Rosa’s part only on the link Rosa sends you.
  • Use a different Rosa part for every website.
  • Keep your phone locked, and protect your mobile carrier account with a PIN or passcode.
  • Turn on two-step verification on websites that offer it, especially for email and banking.
  • Don’t forward Rosa’s links or let others text Rosa as you.

If you save complete passwords, or use Rosa in other ways it isn’t designed for, you do so at your own risk.

What Rosa can’t protect against

We work to keep Rosa secure, and our Privacy Policy describes how. But no service is risk-free, and you should know the limits:

  • Anyone who gets into your phone, your text messages, or your phone number (for example, through SIM swapping) may be able to open Rosa’s links and see the parts you saved.
  • If Rosa’s stored data were ever exposed at the same time as one of your complete passwords from another website, someone could compare the two to work out your private part.
  • Text messages are not end-to-end encrypted.
  • Rosa can’t recover your private part. If you forget it, use the website’s password reset.
  • Rosa doesn’t ask for your name, email address, or usernames, but a phone number can often be linked to a person, so Rosa is not anonymous.
  • Rosa depends on your mobile carrier and on Twilio, our text messaging provider. We aren’t responsible for their outages or mistakes, or for the websites whose passwords you use with Rosa.
